Reading Plan

Romans in 30 days — Paul's fullest case for the gospel.

No New Testament letter builds its argument more carefully than Romans — from universal guilt, to righteousness by faith, to life in the Spirit, to how that gospel reshapes ordinary relationships. This plan works through all 16 chapters in 30 days, mostly by natural paragraph breaks rather than even chapter splits, so each day's reading actually holds together as one thought.

calendar_today 30 Days · All 16 Chapters · BSB Translation

How to use this plan

Read the full passage for the day in your own Bible or inside Formation before you get to the highlight verse below — it's meant to be a place to pause once you arrive there, not a substitute for the reading itself. Sit with the reflection question, and use Formation's notes to write down whatever it surfaces. Romans builds argument on argument, so if a day feels dense, it's worth re-reading rather than pushing ahead to stay on schedule.

1

Romans 1:1–17

Paul introduces himself, states his eagerness to reach Rome, and lays out the letter's thesis: the gospel is God's power for salvation, righteous by faith from first to last.

"I am not ashamed of the gospel, because it is the power of God for salvation to everyone who believes, first to the Jew, then to the Greek." — Romans 1:16, BSB

Reflect: What would it look like this week to actually live like you believe the gospel is “power,” not just information?

2

Romans 1:18–32

God's wrath revealed against human unrighteousness and idolatry, and the downward spiral that follows when people suppress what they know to be true.

"For since the creation of the world God’s invisible qualities, His eternal power and divine nature, have been clearly seen, being understood from His workmanship, so that men are without excuse." — Romans 1:20, BSB

Reflect: Where do you see the pattern of “known but suppressed” truth playing out in your own life, not just in culture at large?

3

Romans 2:1–16

Paul warns against judging others for what you do yourself; God's judgment is impartial, based on truth and what people actually do, not on identity or advantage.

"For God does not show favoritism." — Romans 2:11, BSB

Reflect: Whose behavior do you judge more harshly than you'd want your own judged? Why?

4

Romans 2:17–29

True belonging to God's people was never about outward markers like circumcision, but an inward, Spirit-worked change of heart.

"No, a man is a Jew because he is one inwardly, and circumcision is a matter of the heart, by the Spirit, not by the written code. Such a man’s praise does not come from men, but from God." — Romans 2:29, BSB

Reflect: What outward religious markers do you sometimes lean on in place of an actual inward change?

5

Romans 3:1–20

Jew and Gentile alike stand guilty before God — “there is no one righteous, not even one.” The law exposes sin; it was never able to erase it.

"Therefore no one will be justified in His sight by works of the law. For the law merely brings awareness of sin." — Romans 3:20, BSB

Reflect: What's the difference between the law showing you your sin and the law being able to fix it?

6

Romans 3:21–31

God's righteousness has now been revealed apart from the law, available to everyone who believes, through the redemption accomplished in Christ.

"for all have sinned and fall short of the glory of God, and are justified freely by His grace through the redemption that is in Christ Jesus." — Romans 3:23–24, BSB

Reflect: “Freely by His grace” — where do you still quietly try to earn what this verse says is a gift?

7

Romans 4:1–12

Abraham is Paul's test case: declared righteous by faith before he was ever circumcised, making him the father of everyone who believes, circumcised or not.

"Abraham believed God, and it was credited to him as righteousness." — Romans 4:3, BSB

Reflect: What does it mean that this was credited to Abraham before he'd done anything to earn it?

8

Romans 4:13–25

The promise to Abraham came through faith, not law-keeping — and Abraham's unwavering trust in an impossible promise becomes the pattern for how anyone is credited righteousness today.

"He was delivered over to death for our trespasses and was raised to life for our justification." — Romans 4:25, BSB

Reflect: Abraham “did not waver” even when the promise seemed impossible. What promise are you currently wavering on?

9

Romans 5:1–11

Justified by faith, believers now have peace with God — and can even find a strange kind of joy in suffering, because of what it produces.

"Therefore, since we have been justified through faith, we have peace with God through our Lord Jesus Christ." — Romans 5:1, BSB

Reflect: Does “peace with God” describe your actual, felt experience right now, or mostly a theological fact you know but don't feel?

10

Romans 5:12–21

Adam's one act brought sin and death to everyone; Christ's one act brings grace and life that overflows even further than the damage did.

"The law came in so that the trespass would increase; but where sin increased, grace increased all the more." — Romans 5:20, BSB

Reflect: Where in your life do you need to believe that grace genuinely outpaces the damage, not just covers it?

11

Romans 6:1–14

Grace isn't a license to keep sinning — baptism pictures dying to the old life and being raised to walk in a genuinely new one.

"We were therefore buried with Him through baptism into death, in order that, just as Christ was raised from the dead through the glory of the Father, we too may walk in newness of life." — Romans 6:4, BSB

Reflect: What would “newness of life” look like this week in one specific, ordinary area?

12

Romans 6:15–23

A choice of masters is laid out plainly: slavery to sin leads to death, but slavery to righteousness leads to holiness and eternal life.

"For the wages of sin is death, but the gift of God is eternal life in Christ Jesus our Lord." — Romans 6:23, BSB

Reflect: “Wages” is something earned; “gift” is something given. Which language do you instinctively use for your own standing with God?

13

Romans 7:1–13

The law itself is holy and good, Paul insists — but it exposed sin's power rather than freeing anyone from it.

"So then, the law is holy, and the commandment is holy, righteous, and good." — Romans 7:12, BSB

Reflect: Have you ever blamed the law (or a rule, or a standard) for a problem that was actually sin's fault?

14

Romans 7:14–25

Paul's raw, first-person description of the internal war between wanting to do good and doing what he hates — resolved only through Christ.

"What a wretched man I am! Who will rescue me from this body of death? Thanks be to God, through Jesus Christ our Lord!" — Romans 7:24–25, BSB

Reflect: This is one of the most honest passages in the New Testament. Where do you need that same honesty about your own internal contradictions?

15

Romans 8:1–17

No condemnation for those in Christ; the Spirit sets believers free from sin and death and makes them God's own children.

"Therefore, there is now no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus." — Romans 8:1, BSB

Reflect: Do you actually live like there's “no condemnation,” or do you carry a low-grade sense of it anyway?

16

Romans 8:18–39

Present suffering is real but small next to coming glory — and nothing in all creation can separate believers from the love of God in Christ.

"And we know that God works all things together for the good of those who love Him, who are called according to His purpose." — Romans 8:28, BSB

Reflect: This verse is often quoted alone — read in context, whose “good” is it actually describing, and toward what end (v.29)?

17

Romans 9:1–18

Paul grieves over Israel's unbelief, then traces God's sovereign right to show mercy through the stories of Isaac, Jacob and Esau, and Pharaoh.

"So then, it does not depend on man’s desire or effort, but on God’s mercy." — Romans 9:16, BSB

Reflect: How does it sit with you to read that mercy “does not depend on man's desire or effort”?

18

Romans 9:19–33

The potter-and-clay image answers the “is God unfair?” objection, and Isaiah's stone of stumbling explains why many missed the righteousness they were chasing.

"the one who believes in Him will never be put to shame." — Romans 9:33, BSB

Reflect: Israel pursued righteousness by works and stumbled; what are you tempted to pursue by effort instead of by faith?

19

Romans 10:1–13

Righteousness by faith is available to everyone — Jew or Greek — who calls on the name of the Lord; there's no separate track for anyone.

"that if you confess with your mouth, “Jesus is Lord,” and believe in your heart that God raised Him from the dead, you will be saved." — Romans 10:9, BSB

Reflect: Confession and belief are both named here. Which one comes easier for you, and which one do you avoid?

20

Romans 10:14–21

Faith requires hearing, hearing requires someone preaching, and Israel heard the message but largely did not believe it.

"Consequently, faith comes by hearing, and hearing by the word of Christ." — Romans 10:17, BSB

Reflect: What's currently shaping what you actually hear and believe day to day — is it “the word of Christ,” or something else competing for that seat?

21

Romans 11:1–16

God hasn't rejected His people entirely — there's a remnant chosen by grace, following the same pattern as Elijah's 7,000 who never bowed to Baal.

"And if it is by grace, then it is no longer by works. Otherwise, grace would no longer be grace." — Romans 11:6, BSB

Reflect: Where do you catch yourself quietly mixing grace and works, as if they could blend?

22

Romans 11:17–36

The image of wild branches grafted into a cultivated olive tree, a warning against Gentile arrogance, and a doxology at the depth of God's wisdom in His plan for Jew and Gentile together.

"For from Him and through Him and to Him are all things. To Him be the glory forever! Amen." — Romans 11:36, BSB

Reflect: Paul ends three chapters of hard theology with worship, not just conclusions. What would it look like for your own hard questions to end the same way?

23

Romans 12:1–8

A call to offer your whole self to God as a living sacrifice, to resist being shaped by the world, and to use your particular gifts to serve the rest of the body.

"Do not be conformed to this world, but be transformed by the renewing of your mind." — Romans 12:2, BSB

Reflect: What's one specific way the world has been quietly conforming your mind lately?

24

Romans 12:9–21

A rapid list of practical marks of real love — bless those who persecute you, don't repay evil for evil, live at peace as far as it depends on you.

"Do not be overcome by evil, but overcome evil with good." — Romans 12:21, BSB

Reflect: Where in your life right now is this the harder command — not returning evil, or actively answering it with good?

25

Romans 13:1–7

Instructions on submitting to governing authorities as instituted by God, including paying taxes and giving honor where honor is due.

"Everyone must submit himself to the governing authorities, for there is no authority except that which is from God." — Romans 13:1, BSB

Reflect: How does this passage complicate or clarify how you think about civic life and authority today?

26

Romans 13:8–14

Love fulfills every command of the law in a single stroke; live as people who belong to the day, not the night.

"Love does no wrong to its neighbor. Therefore love is the fulfillment of the law." — Romans 13:10, BSB

Reflect: If love “does no wrong,” what's one relationship where that standard would actually change how you act this week?

27

Romans 14:1–12

Don't judge fellow believers over disputable matters like food and holy days — everyone answers to the Lord, not to each other.

"If we live, we live to the Lord, and if we die, we die to the Lord. So whether we live or die, we belong to the Lord." — Romans 14:8, BSB

Reflect: What disputable matter (not a core doctrine) have you turned into a test of someone else's faithfulness?

28

Romans 14:13–23

Don't let your freedom become a stumbling block for someone weaker in faith; the kingdom of God is bigger than what you eat or drink.

"For the kingdom of God is not a matter of eating and drinking, but of righteousness, peace, and joy in the Holy Spirit." — Romans 14:17, BSB

Reflect: Is there a freedom you have that, exercised carelessly, could genuinely trip someone else up?

29

Romans 15:1–13

The strong are called to bear with the failings of the weak for their good, following Christ's own example, so that Jew and Gentile can praise God together with one voice.

"Now may the God of hope fill you with all joy and peace as you believe in Him, so that you may overflow with hope by the power of the Holy Spirit." — Romans 15:13, BSB

Reflect: What would it take for you to genuinely “bear with” someone weaker, rather than just tolerate them?

30

Romans 15:14–16:27

Paul's travel plans and ministry philosophy, a long list of personal greetings to friends and coworkers in Rome, a final warning about those who cause division, and a closing doxology.

"to the only wise God be glory forever through Jesus Christ! Amen." — Romans 16:27, BSB

Reflect: Thirty days through one letter. What's the single idea from Romans you most need to keep carrying past today?
