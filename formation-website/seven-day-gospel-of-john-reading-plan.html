Reading Plan

Seven days in the Gospel of John — start to empty tomb.

John's Gospel doesn't read like the other three — it opens in eternity before working forward to a Word who "became flesh," then spends its second half almost entirely inside a single week. This plan moves through all 21 chapters in seven days, about three chapters a sitting, ending where John himself says he wrote it all for: "that you may believe."

calendar_today 7 Days · ~3 Chapters / Day · BSB Translation

How to use this plan

Read the full passage for the day in your own Bible or inside Formation. The highlight verse below isn't a substitute for the reading — it's a place to slow down once you get there. Sit with the reflection question before moving on, and use Formation's notes to write down whatever it stirs up. There's no wrong pace; if a day runs long, split it in two rather than skimming to stay on schedule.

1

John 1–3

The Word before time, John the Baptist's testimony, the first sign at Cana, and Jesus telling Nicodemus he must be born again.

"The Word became flesh and made His dwelling among us." — John 1:14, BSB

Reflect: What does it change to read "the Word became flesh" as a historical claim, not just a poetic one?

2

John 4–6

The woman at the well, healing an official's son from a distance, and the feeding of the five thousand followed by the Bread of Life discourse.

"I am the bread of life. Whoever comes to Me will never hunger, and whoever believes in Me will never thirst." — John 6:35, BSB

Reflect: What are you currently looking to for the kind of satisfaction Jesus claims here?

3

John 7–9

Jesus teaches at the Feast of Tabernacles, confronts the crowd's accusations, and heals a man born blind — then confronts the religious leaders' spiritual blindness.

"I am the light of the world. Whoever follows Me will never walk in the darkness, but will have the light of life." — John 8:12, BSB

Reflect: The healed man's honesty grows the more he's pressured. Where might that be true for you?

4

John 10–12

Jesus as the Good Shepherd, the raising of Lazarus, Mary's anointing at Bethany, and the triumphal entry into Jerusalem.

"I am the resurrection and the life. Whoever believes in Me will live, even though he dies." — John 11:25, BSB

Reflect: Jesus weeps at Lazarus's tomb even though He knows what's about to happen. What does that tell you about how He holds grief and hope together?

5

John 13–15

The Last Supper, Jesus washing the disciples' feet, the new commandment, and the extended teaching on the vine and the branches.

"A new commandment I give you: Love one another. As I have loved you, so you also must love one another." — John 13:34, BSB

Reflect: Whose feet, literally or figuratively, is this passage asking you to wash this week?

6

John 16–18

The promise of the Holy Spirit, Jesus's high priestly prayer for His disciples and for all who would believe through them, and His arrest and trial.

"I have told you these things so that in Me you may have peace. In the world you will have tribulation. But take courage; I have overcome the world!" — John 16:33, BSB

Reflect: This promise of peace comes paired with a promise of trouble, not instead of it. How do you usually hold those together?

7

John 19–21

The crucifixion, the empty tomb, Jesus appearing to Mary Magdalene and the disciples, Thomas's doubt answered, and Peter restored by the Sea of Galilee.

"But these are written so that you may believe that Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God, and that by believing you may have life in His name." — John 20:31, BSB

Reflect: John tells you directly why he wrote this book. Has this week's reading moved you toward or away from that stated purpose?
