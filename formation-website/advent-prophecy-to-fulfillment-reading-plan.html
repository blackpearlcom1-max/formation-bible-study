Reading Plan

Prophecy to fulfillment — in fourteen days.

Advent means "arrival." Long before the arrival in Bethlehem, God had already been describing it — through a shepherd-turned-prophet, a grieving exile, and a series of promises spoken hundreds of years apart from each other. Each day in this plan pairs one Old Testament promise with the New Testament moment that answers it, tracing a single thread from Eden's first hint of rescue to Jesus' own words that it has all just come true.

calendar_today 14 Days · Genesis to Luke · BSB Translation

How to use this plan

Each day below pairs a prophecy with its fulfillment — read both passages in full where you can, not just the highlighted verses. Some of these connections span hundreds of years between the promise and the moment it lands; let that distance register rather than rushing past it. This plan works anytime, not only in December — though if you're using it to prepare for Christmas, starting on December 12 lands Day 14 on Christmas Day.

1

The First Promise

Genesis 3:15 → Galatians 4:4 — Immediately after the fall, God speaks the first promise of a rescuer: an offspring of the woman who will crush the serpent's head, at the cost of being wounded Himself. Every prophecy in this plan traces back to this one verse.

"And I will put enmity between you and the woman, and between your seed and her seed. He will crush your head, and you will strike his heel." — Genesis 3:15, BSB

Fulfilled — Galatians 4:4: "But when the time had fully come, God sent His Son, born of a woman, born under the law,"

Reflect: What does it mean that God announced a rescue plan before Adam and Eve even left the garden?

2

The Lamb Provided

Genesis 22:14 → John 1:29 — On the mountain where Abraham expected to sacrifice his own son, God provides a substitute instead, and Abraham names the place after that provision. Two thousand years later, John the Baptist points to Jesus using the very same image.

"And Abraham called that place The LORD Will Provide. So to this day it is said, 'On the mountain of the LORD it will be provided.'" — Genesis 22:14, BSB

Fulfilled — John 1:29: "The next day John saw Jesus coming toward him and said, 'Look, the Lamb of God, who takes away the sin of the world!'"

Reflect: Where in your own life have you needed God to provide a substitute you couldn't provide for yourself?

3

An Eternal Throne

2 Samuel 7:13 → Luke 1:32-33 — God promises David a throne that will never end, a promise no merely human king could keep, since every dynasty eventually falls. Centuries later, the angel Gabriel tells Mary this exact throne, and this exact promise, belongs to her son.

"He will build a house for My Name, and I will establish the throne of his kingdom forever." — 2 Samuel 7:13, BSB

Fulfilled — Luke 1:32: "He will be great and will be called the Son of the Most High. The Lord God will give Him the throne of His father David,"

Reflect: What's the difference between a throne that lasts for a while and one that will never end?

4

God With Us

Isaiah 7:14 → Matthew 1:23 — Isaiah gives a sign to a frightened king: a child will be born and named Immanuel, "God with us." Matthew quotes this verse directly, word for word, to explain who Jesus is before Jesus has said a single thing.

"Therefore the Lord Himself will give you a sign: Behold, the virgin will be with child and will give birth to a son, and will call Him Immanuel." — Isaiah 7:14, BSB

Fulfilled — Matthew 1:23: "Behold, the virgin will be with child and will give birth to a son, and they will call Him Immanuel" (which means, "God with us").

Reflect: Where in your life right now would it help most to remember that God is described as with you, not just for you?

5

Good News of Great Joy

Isaiah 9:6 → Luke 2:10-11 — Isaiah names the coming child with four weighty titles: Wonderful Counselor, Mighty God, Everlasting Father, Prince of Peace. On the night of His actual birth, an angel announces the same reality in far simpler words, to a field of shepherds.

"For unto us a child is born, unto us a son is given, and the government will be upon His shoulders. And He will be called Wonderful Counselor, Mighty God, Everlasting Father, Prince of Peace." — Isaiah 9:6, BSB

Fulfilled — Luke 2:10-11: "For behold, I bring you good news of great joy that will be for all the people: Today in the city of David a Savior has been born to you. He is Christ the Lord!"

Reflect: Which of Isaiah's four titles for this child do you find hardest to actually believe applies to your own life?

6

A Shoot From Jesse's Stump

Isaiah 11:1 → Romans 15:12 — By Isaiah's time, David's family looked more like a cut-down stump than a living dynasty. Isaiah promises new growth from that stump anyway, and Paul later quotes this exact image to explain why Gentiles, not just Jews, can put their hope in Jesus.

"Then a shoot will spring up from the stump of Jesse, and a Branch from his roots will bear fruit." — Isaiah 11:1, BSB

Fulfilled — Romans 15:12: "And once more, Isaiah says: 'The Root of Jesse will appear, One who will arise to rule over the Gentiles; in Him the Gentiles will put their hope.'"

Reflect: What's something in your own life that looks like a stump right now, cut down, with no obvious sign of new growth?

7

The Place

Micah 5:2 → Matthew 2:6 — Micah names the specific, unimpressive town where the ruler of Israel will be born, centuries before anyone could have guessed it. When wise men later ask Herod's scholars where to look, this is the verse they quote back without hesitation.

"But you, Bethlehem Ephrathah, who are small among the clans of Judah, out of you will come forth for Me One to be ruler over Israel — One whose origins are of old, from the days of eternity." — Micah 5:2, BSB

Fulfilled — Matthew 2:6: "But you, Bethlehem, in the land of Judah, are by no means least among the rulers of Judah, for out of you will come a ruler who will be the shepherd of My people Israel."

Reflect: Does it change anything to know God had already named the place, before there was any reason to expect it?

8

A Voice in the Wilderness

Isaiah 40:3 → Luke 3:4 — Isaiah describes a voice preparing the way for the Lord in the wilderness, centuries before there's a wilderness preacher to do it. Luke quotes this verse directly to introduce John the Baptist, identifying him as that voice.

"A voice of one calling: 'Prepare the way for the LORD in the wilderness; make a straight highway for our God in the desert.'" — Isaiah 40:3, BSB

Fulfilled — Luke 3:4: "As it is written in the book of the words of Isaiah the prophet: 'A voice of one calling in the wilderness, "Prepare the way for the Lord, make straight paths for Him."'"

Reflect: What would it look like for you to help "prepare the way" for someone else to encounter God, the way John did?

9

The Messenger

Malachi 3:1 → Luke 1:78-79 — The last book of the Old Testament promises a messenger who will prepare the way before the Lord suddenly comes to His temple. Zechariah's own prophecy over his newborn son John — the same John who becomes the voice in the wilderness — echoes this promise directly.

"Behold, I will send My messenger, who will prepare the way before Me. Then the Lord whom you seek will suddenly come to His temple." — Malachi 3:1, BSB

Fulfilled — Luke 1:78-79: "because of the tender mercy of our God, by which the Dawn will visit us from on high, to shine on those who sit in darkness and in the shadow of death, to guide our feet into the way of peace."

Reflect: Malachi's messenger prepares the way; John's father calls his son a light for those who sit in darkness. Who in your life might need you to be that kind of messenger?

10

Called Out of Egypt

Hosea 11:1 → Matthew 2:15 — Hosea recalls God calling Israel, His son, out of Egyptian slavery generations earlier. Matthew applies the same words to the infant Jesus, whose family flees to Egypt and later returns — a small life recapitulating Israel's larger story.

"When Israel was a child, I loved him, and out of Egypt I called My son." — Hosea 11:1, BSB

Fulfilled — Matthew 2:15: "So he got up, took the Child and His mother by night, and withdrew to Egypt... This fulfilled what the Lord had spoken through the prophet: 'Out of Egypt I called My Son.'"

Reflect: What does it tell you about how God works that Jesus' own life retraces Israel's history in miniature?

11

Rachel's Grief

Jeremiah 31:15 → Matthew 2:18 — Jeremiah pictures the matriarch Rachel weeping inconsolably for children lost generations before, during Israel's exile. Matthew applies the same image to the mothers of Bethlehem after Herod's massacre of the infants — real grief that Scripture doesn't rush past or explain away.

"This is what the LORD says: 'A voice is heard in Ramah, mourning and great weeping, Rachel weeping for her children, and refusing consolation, because they are no more.'" — Jeremiah 31:15, BSB

Fulfilled — Matthew 2:18: "A voice is heard in Ramah, weeping and great mourning, Rachel weeping for her children and refusing to be comforted, because they are no more."

Reflect: This is the one day in the plan where the "fulfillment" is tragedy, not triumph. What does it mean that Scripture makes room for grief like this without immediately resolving it?

12

A Light Dawns

Isaiah 9:2 → Matthew 4:16 — Isaiah promises light for a specific region living in darkness — the same region, Galilee, where Jesus grows up and begins His public ministry generations later. Matthew quotes the verse directly as Jesus starts preaching there.

"The people walking in darkness have seen a great light; on those living in the land of the shadow of death, a light has dawned." — Isaiah 9:2, BSB

Fulfilled — Matthew 4:16: "the people living in darkness have seen a great light; on those living in the land of the shadow of death, a light has dawned."

Reflect: Where in your life right now feels most like "the land of the shadow of death" — and what would it look like for light to dawn there?

13

Pierced for Us

Isaiah 53:5 → 1 Peter 2:24 — Isaiah's servant song describes a coming figure pierced for others' wrongdoing, crushed for others' guilt, whose wounds bring others healing — written centuries before crucifixion existed as a method of execution. Peter applies this description directly to the cross.

"But He was pierced for our transgressions, He was crushed for our iniquities; the punishment that brought us peace was upon Him, and by His stripes we are healed." — Isaiah 53:5, BSB

Fulfilled — 1 Peter 2:24: "He Himself bore our sins in His body on the tree, so that we might die to sin and live to righteousness. 'By His stripes you are healed.'"

Reflect: Isaiah 53 was written for people who didn't yet know how it would be fulfilled. What does it mean to trust a promise before you can see how it resolves?

14

Fulfilled in Your Hearing

Isaiah 61:1 → Luke 4:21 — Isaiah describes a coming figure anointed to bring good news to the poor, freedom to captives, and comfort to the brokenhearted. In His hometown synagogue, Jesus reads this exact passage aloud, rolls up the scroll, sits down, and says — in the plainest possible terms — that it has just been fulfilled.

"The Spirit of the Lord GOD is on Me, because the LORD has anointed Me to preach good news to the poor. He has sent Me to bind up the brokenhearted, to proclaim liberty to the captives and freedom to the prisoners," — Isaiah 61:1, BSB

Fulfilled — Luke 4:21: "and He began by saying, 'Today this Scripture is fulfilled in your hearing.'"

Reflect: After thirteen days of promises spoken centuries apart from their fulfillment, Jesus simply says: today. What difference does it make to encounter these prophecies as already fulfilled, not still pending?
