A session template you can reuse

Every session in the Romans guide follows the same four-part shape. It travels to almost any passage:

  1. Icebreaker (5–10 min) — a low-stakes question that gets everyone talking before the text is even open.
  2. Discussion questions (20–30 min) — observation questions first ("what does the text say"), then interpretation, then application — see Writing Better Discussion Questions for the full three-layer framework.
  3. Leader note — one piece of background or context the leader brings that the group likely wouldn't find on its own.
  4. Closing prompt — a single question or action step members carry into the week, not a summary of everything covered.

Six icebreaker ideas

  • "What's one word you'd use to describe your week?" — low-stakes, gets every voice in the room early.
  • "Read today's passage title/theme — what do you expect this to be about, before we read it?"
  • "What's a rule or expectation you followed as a kid without ever being told why?" (works well before a passage on obedience or law)
  • "Name one thing you're genuinely thankful for this week, no matter how small."
  • "If you could ask the author of this book one question, what would it be?"
  • "What's something you used to believe about this topic that's since changed?"

Common questions

Do I need leadership experience to run a group?

No. These guides exist specifically because most first-time leaders were never taught how — just asked to volunteer. Start with the session template above and the Romans guide as a full worked example.

Can I use these guides for an in-person group, or only inside the app?

Both. The guides themselves are plain reading — print them, read them on your phone before a meeting, or use Formation's Groups feature to share material and recaps with your group directly.

What if my group wants to study a book that doesn't have a dedicated group guide yet?

Use the session template above with any book guide as your source material — the four-part shape (icebreaker, discussion questions, leader note, closing prompt) travels to almost any passage or book.
