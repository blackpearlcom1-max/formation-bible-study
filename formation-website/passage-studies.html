The editorial standard behind every passage study

Every study below follows the same seven-part structure, whether the passage is three verses or fourteen:

1. The Passage

The full text in the Berean Standard Bible.

2. The Observation

One specific question about what's actually on the page, asked before any interpretation.

3. Direct Scripture

The verse itself, quoted precisely, with nothing added.

4. Cross-References

Related passages elsewhere in Scripture that speak to the same idea.

5. Lexical Context

What the original Hebrew or Greek word actually carries, and where else it appears.

6. Commentary

How the passage has broadly been read historically — named as one common line of interpretation, not the only one.

7. Disputed View (where relevant)

Where sincere Christians read the passage differently, both readings are named side by side — followed by a saved note and a group discussion prompt you can bring to your own study.

This isn't a style choice — it's the same structure Ask the Word uses when you ask it a question directly inside the app, so the sourcing is always visible rather than asked to be taken on faith.

Common questions

Why these seven passages specifically?

Each is a well-known verse or short passage that's frequently quoted out of its immediate context — "I can do all things," "no condemnation," "love never fails." The full study restores the context the popular quotation usually leaves out.

Can I do a passage study on a verse that isn't listed here?

Yes — every verse in Formation supports the same observation, evidence, and note-taking tools used to build the studies above. This page collects the fully-written examples; the method itself works on any passage you open.

What's the difference between "Disputed View" here and the Compare Interpretations page?

Disputed View flags disagreement inside a single passage study, in a few sentences. Compare Interpretations is a dedicated, full-length comparison for the handful of questions where the disagreement is a whole doctrine, not just one verse's nuance.

How long does a full passage study take to work through?

Most readers spend fifteen to twenty-five minutes on a full study — longer than a quick devotional read, shorter than a full book guide. The seven parts are designed to be read in one sitting, though nothing stops you from returning to the evidence sections later.
