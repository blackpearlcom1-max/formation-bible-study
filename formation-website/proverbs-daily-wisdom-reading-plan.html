Reading Plan

Proverbs: daily wisdom — one chapter a day, for a month.

Proverbs has 31 chapters — almost exactly one for every day of an average month. This plan follows that natural rhythm: one full chapter a day, straight through, from the book's opening charge to fear the LORD to its closing portrait of a woman of noble character.

calendar_today 31 Days · All 31 Chapters · BSB Translation

How to use this plan

Read the full chapter for the day in your own Bible or inside Formation — the highlight verse below is a place to pause, not a substitute for the whole chapter. Proverbs is meant to be practical, so try picking one specific thing from the chapter to actually apply that same day, not just read and move on. Use Formation's notes to track which proverbs come up again and again in your own life.

1

Proverbs 1

The purpose of the whole book stated up front: gaining wisdom starts with reverence for God, not raw intelligence.

"The fear of the LORD is the beginning of knowledge, but fools despise wisdom and discipline." — Proverbs 1:7, BSB

Reflect: What would change if you treated “the fear of the LORD” as the starting point for wisdom, rather than something to get to eventually?

2

Proverbs 2

Wisdom is framed as a gift that comes from God's mouth, not something you can manufacture through sheer effort alone.

"For the LORD gives wisdom; from His mouth come knowledge and understanding." — Proverbs 2:6, BSB

Reflect: Where are you currently trying to generate wisdom on your own instead of simply asking God for it?

3

Proverbs 3

The most quoted verse in the whole book: trust God over your own understanding, in every decision, not just the hard ones.

"Trust in the LORD with all your heart, and lean not on your own understanding; in all your ways acknowledge Him, and He will make your paths straight." — Proverbs 3:5–6, BSB

Reflect: Where in your life are you currently leaning on your own understanding instead of actually trusting God with it?

4

Proverbs 4

A father's urgent charge to his son: guard your heart above everything else, because every other part of life flows from it.

"Guard your heart with all diligence, for from it flow springs of life." — Proverbs 4:23, BSB

Reflect: What are you letting into your heart right now that you wouldn't want shaping everything else in your life?

5

Proverbs 5

A warning against the forbidden woman, grounded in the sober reminder that every path — even the hidden ones — is seen by God.

"For a man's ways are before the eyes of the LORD, and the LORD examines all his paths." — Proverbs 5:21, BSB

Reflect: Does it change your choices to remember your ways are “before the eyes of the LORD,” even the ones no one else sees?

6

Proverbs 6

The sluggard is sent to learn from the ant's diligence, alongside warnings against pride, adultery, and the schemes God hates.

"Walk in the manner of the ant, O slacker; observe its ways and become wise." — Proverbs 6:6, BSB

Reflect: What's one area where you need the ant's example — working diligently without being told, watched, or supervised?

7

Proverbs 7

A vivid case study of seduction and its aftermath, framed as a call to hold wisdom's words as closely as your own eyesight.

"Keep my commandments and live; guard my teachings as the apple of your eye." — Proverbs 7:2, BSB

Reflect: What piece of wisdom have you heard but never actually guarded closely enough for it to matter?

8

Proverbs 8

Wisdom personified, calling out publicly in the streets, valued above every treasure a person could chase instead.

"For wisdom is more precious than rubies, and nothing you desire compares with her." — Proverbs 8:11, BSB

Reflect: If wisdom really is “more precious than rubies,” what are you currently valuing above it?

9

Proverbs 9

Two houses stand side by side — Wisdom's and Folly's — both issuing the same invitation to the same simple people.

"The fear of the LORD is the beginning of wisdom, and knowledge of the Holy One is understanding." — Proverbs 9:10, BSB

Reflect: Which invitation have you been listening to more lately — wisdom's or folly's?

10

Proverbs 10

The first of Solomon's short couplets, contrasting wise and foolish living line by line, often in the same breath.

"Hatred stirs up dissension, but love covers all transgressions." — Proverbs 10:12, BSB

Reflect: Where is old resentment currently “stirring up dissension” in you that love could cover instead?

11

Proverbs 11

Generosity leads to real prosperity, while hoarding — despite feeling safer — actually leads to loss.

"A generous soul will prosper, and he who refreshes others will himself be refreshed." — Proverbs 11:25, BSB

Reflect: What's one specific way you could “refresh others” this week, trusting you'll be refreshed yourself?

12

Proverbs 12

The wisdom of actually receiving correction, instead of the fool's instinct to dismiss it outright.

"The way of a fool is right in his own eyes, but a wise man listens to counsel." — Proverbs 12:15, BSB

Reflect: When was the last time you genuinely changed your mind because of someone else's counsel?

13

Proverbs 13

Wisdom is something you catch from your company as much as something you're taught — good or bad, both rub off.

"He who walks with the wise will become wise, but the companion of fools will be destroyed." — Proverbs 13:20, BSB

Reflect: Who are you walking closely with right now, and what are you slowly becoming because of it?

14

Proverbs 14

A sober warning that a path can feel completely right to you and still end in death.

"There is a way that seems right to a man, but its end is the way of death." — Proverbs 14:12, BSB

Reflect: What decision are you currently justifying because it “feels right” that this verse is asking you to examine more closely?

15

Proverbs 15

The power of tone, not just content — how something is said can defuse or ignite the exact same conversation.

"A gentle answer turns away wrath, but a harsh word stirs up anger." — Proverbs 15:1, BSB

Reflect: Where has a harsh answer of yours recently stirred up more anger than the situation actually called for?

16

Proverbs 16

A call to commit your plans to God rather than simply assuming your own judgment is sufficient on its own.

"Commit your works to the LORD and your plans will be achieved." — Proverbs 16:3, BSB

Reflect: What's a plan you're currently working out entirely on your own that you haven't actually committed to God?

17

Proverbs 17

Friendship defined by constancy — especially the kind that shows up in hardship, not just in good times.

"A friend loves at all times, and a brother is born for adversity." — Proverbs 17:17, BSB

Reflect: Who has been “a friend at all times” for you, and have you actually told them that?

18

Proverbs 18

God's name pictured as a fortified tower — a place to run to for real safety, not just a title to acknowledge.

"The name of the LORD is a strong tower; the righteous run to it and are safe." — Proverbs 18:10, BSB

Reflect: What are you currently running to for safety instead of running to God first?

19

Proverbs 19

Human planning laid alongside God's ultimate purpose, which prevails no matter how carefully we've mapped things out.

"Many plans are in a man's heart, but the purpose of the LORD will prevail." — Proverbs 19:21, BSB

Reflect: What plan are you holding loosely enough to let God's purpose override, if it needs to?

20

Proverbs 20

Integrity described as a legacy that outlives you, quietly shaping the people who come after you.

"The righteous man walks with integrity; blessed are his children after him." — Proverbs 20:7, BSB

Reflect: What kind of legacy is your current integrity — or lack of it — quietly building for the people who come after you?

21

Proverbs 21

A caution against self-justification: it's easy to assume your own motives are pure while God is weighing the heart underneath.

"All a man's ways seem right to him, but the LORD weighs the heart." — Proverbs 21:2, BSB

Reflect: Where might you be assuming your own motives are fine just because your actions look fine on the outside?

22

Proverbs 22

The most familiar parenting verse in Proverbs — early formation setting a direction that tends to last a lifetime.

"Train up a child in the way he should go, and when he is old he will not depart from it." — Proverbs 22:6, BSB

Reflect: What are you actively “training” into the people who watch you, whether you're a parent or not?

23

Proverbs 23

A father's counsel on self-control and patience, closing with a promise worth holding onto in hard seasons.

"For surely there is a future, and your hope will not be cut off." — Proverbs 23:18, BSB

Reflect: Where do you need to hold onto “your hope will not be cut off” right now?

24

Proverbs 24

A picture of resilience after failure — the issue was never falling, it's staying down.

"For though a righteous man may fall seven times, he still gets up; but the wicked stumble in bad times." — Proverbs 24:16, BSB

Reflect: What's one specific way you could “get back up” this week from a recent failure instead of staying down?

25

Proverbs 25

The craft of a well-timed word, compared here to a rare and beautifully made gift rather than something cheap or common.

"A word fitly spoken is like apples of gold in settings of silver." — Proverbs 25:11, BSB

Reflect: When was the last time your words were genuinely “fitly spoken” — the right thing at the right moment?

26

Proverbs 26

A close, unflinching look at folly, including the tricky judgment call of when and how to respond to a fool at all.

"Do not answer a fool according to his folly, or you yourself will be like him." — Proverbs 26:4, BSB

Reflect: Is there a current argument you're in that this verse is warning you not to get pulled further into?

27

Proverbs 27

Friendship's sharpening effect on us, even when the friction of it doesn't feel pleasant in the moment.

"As iron sharpens iron, so one man sharpens another." — Proverbs 27:17, BSB

Reflect: Who in your life sharpens you, even when it doesn't feel good at the time?

28

Proverbs 28

The relief that comes from confession, contrasted with the exhausting work of keeping something concealed.

"He who conceals his sins will not prosper, but whoever confesses and renounces them will find mercy." — Proverbs 28:13, BSB

Reflect: What's something you're currently concealing that this verse is inviting you to confess instead?

29

Proverbs 29

A people without vision or revelation described as running wild — contrasted with the blessing of keeping God's law.

"Where there is no vision, the people cast off restraint; but blessed is he who keeps the Law." — Proverbs 29:18, BSB

Reflect: Where in your life do you feel like you're “running wild” right now for lack of a clear vision from God?

30

Proverbs 30

Agur's humble prayer for neither poverty nor riches, asking instead for exactly enough to stay honestly dependent on God.

"Keep falsehood and deceitful words far from me. Give me neither poverty nor riches; feed me with the bread that is my portion." — Proverbs 30:8, BSB

Reflect: Would you actually pray Agur's prayer — neither poverty nor riches — or do you secretly want one extreme more than the other?

31

Proverbs 31

The book's closing portrait of a woman of noble character, ending not on outward beauty but on reverence for God.

"Charm is deceptive and beauty is fleeting, but a woman who fears the LORD is to be praised." — Proverbs 31:30, BSB

Reflect: Where are you currently prioritizing charm or appearance over the kind of character this chapter actually praises?
