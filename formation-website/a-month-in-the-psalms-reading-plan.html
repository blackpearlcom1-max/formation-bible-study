Reading Plan

A month in the Psalms — praise, lament, and everything between.

The Psalter has 150 chapters and every human emotion in it — this plan doesn't try to cover all of them. Instead it's a curated tour of 30 psalms, opening at Psalm 1 and closing at Psalm 150, chosen to span the range: confident trust, raw lament, repentance, thanksgiving, wisdom, and unrestrained praise. One psalm a day for 30 days.

How to use this plan

Read the full psalm for the day in your own Bible or inside Formation — the highlight verse below is a place to pause, not a substitute for the whole poem. Psalms were written to be prayed as much as read, so consider praying the psalm back to God in your own words before moving to the reflection question. Use Formation's notes to keep track of which psalms you return to most.

1

Psalm 1

The Two Paths: a life rooted in God's law is like a fruitful, well-watered tree; the wicked, by contrast, are like chaff the wind blows away.

"Blessed is the man who does not walk in the counsel of the wicked, or set foot on the path of sinners, or sit in the seat of mockers. But his delight is in the Law of the LORD, and on His law he meditates day and night." — Psalm 1:1–2, BSB

Reflect: What would it look like this week to actually “meditate day and night” on something true, rather than just consuming it once?

2

Psalm 8

A meditation on humanity's small size next to the vastness of creation — and the wonder that God is mindful of us anyway.

"When I behold Your heavens, the work of Your fingers, the moon and the stars, which You have set in place — what is man that You are mindful of him, or the son of man that You care for him?" — Psalm 8:3–4, BSB

Reflect: Where in your life do you need a fresh sense of your own smallness — and God's attentiveness anyway?

3

Psalm 19

Creation itself proclaims God's glory without a single word, and God's law is described as perfect, reviving the soul.

"The heavens declare the glory of God; the skies proclaim the work of His hands." — Psalm 19:1, BSB

Reflect: What has creation itself been “declaring” to you lately that you've been too busy to notice?

4

Psalm 22

A raw cry of abandonment that turns into praise and prophetic hope — the psalm Jesus quoted from the cross.

"My God, my God, why have You forsaken me? Why are You so far from saving me, so far from my words of groaning?" — Psalm 22:1, BSB

Reflect: Have you ever prayed something as honest as “why have You forsaken me”? What happened when you did?

5

Psalm 23

The most familiar psalm of trust: God pictured as a shepherd who provides, guides, and protects through the darkest valley.

"The LORD is my shepherd; I shall not want." — Psalm 23:1, BSB

Reflect: Which phrase from this psalm do you most need right now — “I shall not want,” “He restores my soul,” or “I will fear no evil”?

6

Psalm 24

A liturgy of ascent — first asking who may stand in God's presence, then calling the gates themselves to open for the King of glory.

"Lift up your heads, O gates, be lifted up, O ancient doors, that the King of glory may enter! Who is this King of glory? The LORD strong and mighty, the LORD mighty in battle." — Psalm 24:7–8, BSB

Reflect: What would it mean to actually “lift up the gates” of your own life and let God in fully, not partially?

7

Psalm 27

Confident trust in God as light and salvation even while surrounded by enemies, paired with a longing simply to dwell in His presence.

"The LORD is my light and my salvation — whom shall I fear? The LORD is the stronghold of my life — whom shall I dread?" — Psalm 27:1, BSB

Reflect: What are you currently more afraid of than you are confident in God?

8

Psalm 32

David's testimony of the relief that comes after confessing sin, instead of carrying its weight in silence.

"Blessed is he whose transgressions are forgiven, whose sins are covered." — Psalm 32:1, BSB

Reflect: Is there something you're currently carrying in silence that this psalm is inviting you to confess instead?

9

Psalm 34

Praise born out of real deliverance, inviting others to “taste and see” God's goodness for themselves rather than just take his word for it.

"Taste and see that the LORD is good; blessed is the man who takes refuge in Him!" — Psalm 34:8, BSB

Reflect: What's one specific way you could “taste and see” God's goodness this week, not just affirm it as true?

10

Psalm 37

A wisdom psalm counseling patience and trust instead of envy toward the wicked, whose apparent success won't last.

"Delight yourself in the LORD, and He will give you the desires of your heart." — Psalm 37:4, BSB

Reflect: Where does envy of someone else's apparent success quietly show up in you?

11

Psalm 40

David recalls being lifted out of the pit and given a new song, then offers himself in obedience rather than empty ritual.

"I waited patiently for the LORD; He inclined to me and heard my cry." — Psalm 40:1, BSB

Reflect: What “pit” has God lifted you out of, and have you actually sung the new song yet, or just moved on?

12

Psalm 42

An honest psalm of spiritual thirst and discouragement, repeatedly preaching hope back to a downcast soul.

"As the deer pants for streams of water, so my soul longs after You, O God." — Psalm 42:1, BSB

Reflect: Which line describes you more today — “my soul thirsts for God” or “why are you downcast, O my soul”? Maybe both?

13

Psalm 46

God as an unshakeable refuge even if the earth itself gives way, climaxing in the command to “be still, and know that I am God.”

"God is our refuge and strength, an ever-present help in times of trouble." — Psalm 46:1, BSB

Reflect: Where do you need to hear “be still” this week, specifically?

14

Psalm 51

David's prayer of repentance after his sin with Bathsheba, asking God to create a clean heart rather than just erase the record.

"Create in me a clean heart, O God, and renew a right spirit within me." — Psalm 51:10, BSB

Reflect: Is there a “clean heart” prayer you've been avoiding because it would mean facing something specific?

15

Psalm 62

A picture of waiting on God alone, rather than divided trust in wealth, status, or human strength.

"In God alone my soul finds rest; my salvation comes from Him. He alone is my rock and my salvation. He is my fortress; I will never be shaken." — Psalm 62:1–2, BSB

Reflect: What are you currently trusting in that isn't God alone?

16

Psalm 63

Written in the wilderness, an intense, physical longing for God's presence that outweighs even basic needs.

"O God, You are my God. Earnestly I seek You; my soul thirsts for You." — Psalm 63:1, BSB

Reflect: What would “earnestly seeking” God look like for you today, physically and not just mentally?

17

Psalm 67

A short prayer for blessing whose real purpose is stated plainly: that God's ways would become known to every nation, not just this one.

"Let the peoples praise You, O God; let all the peoples praise You." — Psalm 67:3, BSB

Reflect: How does it change your view of blessing to read that its purpose is other nations knowing God, not just your own comfort?

18

Psalm 73

Asaph's honest struggle with watching the wicked prosper, resolved only once he enters God's presence and sees the bigger picture.

"Whom have I in heaven but You? And on earth I desire no one besides You. My flesh and my heart may fail, but God is the strength of my heart and my portion forever." — Psalm 73:25–26, BSB

Reflect: Have you ever nearly let someone else's apparent success shake your own faith? What brought you back?

19

Psalm 84

A pilgrim's longing for the temple, valuing nearness to God above comfort, status, or security anywhere else.

"For better is one day in Your courts than a thousand elsewhere." — Psalm 84:10, BSB

Reflect: What are you currently trading nearness to God for — comfort, status, or something else?

20

Psalm 90

Moses reflects on the brevity of human life against God's eternity, and asks for wisdom to number our days well.

"So teach us to number our days, that we may present a heart of wisdom." — Psalm 90:12, BSB

Reflect: What would it mean to actually ask God for a “heart of wisdom” about how you're spending your time?

21

Psalm 91

A psalm of protection and refuge for anyone who makes God their dwelling place, using vivid imagery of shelter, shadow, and shield.

"He who dwells in the shelter of the Most High will abide in the shadow of the Almighty. I will say to the LORD, “You are my refuge and my fortress, my God, in whom I trust.”" — Psalm 91:1–2, BSB

Reflect: Where do you currently need to remember that God neither slumbers nor sleeps?

22

Psalm 95

A call to worship that turns into a warning: don't harden your hearts the way a previous generation did in the wilderness.

"Come, let us sing for joy to the LORD; let us shout to the Rock of our salvation! Let us enter His presence with thanksgiving; let us make a joyful noise to Him in song." — Psalm 95:1–2, BSB

Reflect: Where might you be “hardening your heart” the way this psalm warns against?

23

Psalm 100

A short, joyful call to enter God's presence with thanksgiving, grounded in the simple fact that He made us and is good.

"Enter His gates with thanksgiving and His courts with praise; give thanks to Him and bless His name." — Psalm 100:4, BSB

Reflect: What would it look like to enter this specific day “with thanksgiving,” starting right now?

24

Psalm 103

A sweeping catalog of God's benefits — forgiveness, healing, compassion, patience — met with a deliberate call to bless His name.

"Bless the LORD, O my soul; all that is within me, bless His holy name. Bless the LORD, O my soul, and do not forget all His kind deeds." — Psalm 103:1–2, BSB

Reflect: Which of God's “benefits” listed here have you forgotten lately?

25

Psalm 116

Thanksgiving for God hearing and answering a desperate prayer, with a resolve to keep calling on Him for the rest of life.

"I love the LORD, for He has heard my voice — my appeal for mercy." — Psalm 116:1, BSB

Reflect: What's a specific prayer God has already answered that you haven't properly thanked Him for?

26

Psalm 121

A pilgrim's song of confidence that the Maker of heaven and earth is also a personal, never-sleeping protector.

"I lift up my eyes to the hills. From where does my help come? My help comes from the LORD, the Maker of heaven and earth." — Psalm 121:1–2, BSB

Reflect: Where do you need a Protector who “will not slumber” right now?

27

Psalm 130

A cry from real depths, moving from confession to hope, ending with a call for the whole people of God to wait on the Lord.

"Out of the depths I cry to You, O LORD!" — Psalm 130:1, BSB

Reflect: What are the “depths” you're currently crying out from, and have you brought them honestly to God?

28

Psalm 139

God's total knowledge of a person, from their thoughts to their formation in the womb, met with awe rather than fear.

"I praise You, for I am fearfully and wonderfully made. Marvelous are Your works, and I know this very well." — Psalm 139:14, BSB

Reflect: Does “fearfully and wonderfully made” describe how you actually see yourself, or is there a gap?

29

Psalm 145

A psalm of praise cataloging God's greatness, goodness, and nearness to everyone who calls on Him in truth.

"Great is the LORD and greatly to be praised; His greatness is unsearchable." — Psalm 145:3, BSB

Reflect: What's one specific way God's nearness to “all who call on Him” has shown up in your own life?

30

Psalm 150

The Psalter's closing shout: every instrument, every voice, everything that breathes, called to praise the LORD.

"Let everything that has breath praise the LORD! Hallelujah!" — Psalm 150:6, BSB

Reflect: If this were the last thing you ever read, would “let everything that has breath praise the LORD” be a fitting way to close it?
