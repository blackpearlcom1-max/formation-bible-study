Under the Hood

How it works, step by step

No mystery. This is exactly what happens when you ask a question.

1

You ask a question in plain language

Type it the way you'd ask a trusted pastor — "What does John 1:1 mean when it says 'the Word was God'?" or "What is the connection between Passover and the Last Supper?"

2

Formation searches the BSB text first

Your question is matched against the full Berean Standard Bible — locating every passage with direct relevance. Scripture is always Step One. Nothing else overrules it.

3

It retrieves from 2,400 years of curated commentary

Formation searches a curated body of scholarly commentary — Church Fathers, the Reformation tradition, and trusted modern evangelical scholarship — to surface how faithful interpreters across history have understood this passage.

4

You receive a grounded, cited answer

The response you see is built from retrieved material — not invented text. You can trace every point back to the BSB or to the scholarly tradition it came from. Your study stays yours.

Common Questions

Still have questions?

Does Ask the Word use ChatGPT or a general AI? expand_more
No. Formation uses a purpose-built system trained specifically for biblical retrieval — not a general-purpose chatbot. Unlike ChatGPT, it is constrained to the BSB text and our curated scholarly source library. It can still misread a question or source, so every answer includes citations you can verify against the text yourself.
What if Ask the Word gets something wrong? expand_more
Every response should be measured against the text of Scripture itself — and that is exactly how Formation is designed to be used. Formation is a study tool, and every serious Bible student checks their sources. If you find a response that seems inaccurate, you can flag it in the app and our team reviews it. This is part of how we continuously improve the source library.
Is this safe for new or young believers? expand_more
Yes — with one important caveat. Ask the Word surfaces real answers from the orthodox tradition, which means it may surface nuanced theological positions on difficult passages. We encourage all users, especially newer believers, to bring what they find to their pastor or small group for discussion. Formation is designed to deepen your engagement with Scripture and your community — not replace either one.
